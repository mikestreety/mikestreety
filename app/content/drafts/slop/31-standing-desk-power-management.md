A standing desk changes how you think about cables. On a normal desk, cables can hang down the back and nobody cares. On a desk that moves 50cm up and down several times a day, every cable that runs from the desk to the wall is something that can be pulled tight, snagged or yanked out of a socket.

The aim is simple: **one cable from the desk to the wall**.

## Mount the power on the desk

Everything on the desk (monitors, laptop charger, dock, lamp, speakers) should plug into an extension lead fixed to the underside of the desk. That way, when the desk moves, the power moves with it.

A couple of things to watch:

- **Check the total load.** Add up what's plugged in. Monitors and a laptop are fine, but a heater or kettle on the same lead isn't.
- **Don't daisy-chain extension leads.** If you need more sockets, get a bigger lead.
- **Leave the desk's own motor plugged into the wall**, or into the same lead if its manual allows. Check the manual.

## Use a cable tray

A cable tray under the desk holds the extension lead, the power bricks and any spare cable length. Power bricks are the worst offenders for dangling, and a tray hides them completely.

## One cable down to the floor

From the tray, a single power cable (plus network, if you're wired) runs down to the wall. Give it enough slack for the desk at its highest, and run it through a cable spine or sleeve so it folds neatly when the desk comes down.

Raise the desk to its full height before you cut cable ties or fix anything. It's much easier to find out the cable's too short now than when you're standing at it.
